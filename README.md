# WCVI Ecopath
[![DOI](https://zenodo.org/badge/DOI/10.5281/zenodo.20469726.svg)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20469726)
A balanced Ecopath model of the West Coast of Vancouver Island (WCVI) ecosystem representing average ecosystem conditions during the early 1990s.

This repository contains the standalone Ecopath model developed as part of my PhD research at the Institute for the Oceans and Fisheries, University of British Columbia. The model represents the food web structure of the WCVI coastal upwelling ecosystem and was developed as the higher-trophic-level component of the WCVI-E2E end-to-end ecosystem modelling framework.

## Overview
Marine ecosystems are shaped by complex trophic interactions linking primary producers, zooplankton, fish, marine mammals, seabirds, fisheries, and detrital pathways. Ecopath provides a mass-balanced snapshot of these trophic relationships and serves as a foundation for ecosystem analyses and dynamic ecosystem modelling.

This WCVI Ecopath model represents average ecosystem conditions during the early 1990s and includes functional groups spanning the entire food web, from phytoplankton to top predators. The model was developed to:

- Characterize the trophic structure of the WCVI ecosystem;
- Quantify energy flows throughout the food web;
- Evaluate ecosystem structure and functioning;
- Assess the ecological role of key functional groups;
- Provide the higher trophic level component for the WCVI-E2E end-to-end ecosystem model.

## Scientific Contributions

Key contributions of this model include:

- Comprehensive representation of the WCVI coastal food web;
- Mass-balanced Ecopath model spanning lower to upper trophic levels;
- Updated parameterization and diet composition data for WCVI functional groups;
- Ecosystem-level indicators describing food-web structure and functioning;
- Mixed Trophic Impact (MTI) analyses;
- Fisheries representation and trophic analysis of fishing impacts;
- Foundation for the development of the WCVI-E2E end-to-end ecosystem model;
- Openly archived ecosystem model available for reuse and comparative ecosystem studies.

## Study Area

The model represents the marine ecosystem of the West Coast of Vancouver Island (WCVI), British Columbia, Canada.

The WCVI is a productive coastal upwelling ecosystem influenced by seasonal wind-driven upwelling, ocean circulation, and strong bottom-up trophic linkages. The region supports commercially important fisheries and diverse marine communities including forage fish, groundfish, marine mammals, and seabirds.
